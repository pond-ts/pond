# Wind direction

A year of hourly wind reports, binned into the sixteen compass sectors. The x
axis here isn't a number line and isn't time — it's **sixteen named slots in a
fixed order**, which is what `<CategoryAxis>` draws. Every distribution-shaped
question ends up in this shape: browsers, error codes, regions, causes.

## The chart

  <GalleryWindRose width={640} />
</ChartExample>

Hover a bar and the axis names the sector under the pointer. That's what
`cursor="crosshair"` does on an **ordinal** axis: a vertical line plus the
category's name, and no horizontal arm or value pill, because there's no
continuous x position to read back. It earns its place here — the axis only has
room to print eight of the sixteen labels, so half of them are otherwise
unnamed.

Seattle-Tacoma sits in a north-south trough between two mountain ranges, and
the distribution says so: the wind comes from the south a third of the time
(**32.9%** across S, SSW and SSE) and from the north **19.2%**, with the
east-west sectors thin. The single biggest slot for the year is S at 13.8%,
with SSW a hair behind at 13.7%.

The Gallery card steps through the twelve months, and the whole distribution
swings as it goes. **October** is the most southerly month of 2024, at 45.2%;
**July is the one month that reverses**, 31.1% northerly against 16.5%
southerly, as the summer sea breeze sets up from the opposite end of the
trough.

## The data

**Real, measured, public domain.** Hourly METAR (`FM-15`) observations for WBAN
station `72793024233` — the same Seattle-Tacoma airport as the
[temperature](./temperature-range.mdx) and [rainfall](./rainfall.mdx) cards —
for calendar 2024, from
[NOAA NCEI's Local Climatological Data service](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1).
A US federal government work, so no copyright attaches.

**8,735 hourly observations**, binned **offline** into 16 sectors × 12 months
by
[`website/scripts/fixtures/weather.mjs`](https://github.com/pond-ts/pond/blob/main/website/scripts/fixtures/weather.mjs).
Shipping every hour to the browser to draw sixteen bars would be silly, and the
binning isn't the lesson here.

Two categories have no direction to plot, and rather than being folded into a
sector or quietly dropped they are counted separately:

| Category     | Hours | Share |
| ------------ | ----: | ----: |
| **Calm**     |   621 |  7.1% |
| **Variable** |   315 |  3.6% |

A calm hour is reported as direction 000 with zero speed; a variable one is
METAR's `VRB`, a direction shifting faster than the observation resolves.
Both stay in the **denominator** — the sixteen bars are a share of _all_
observed hours, not of directed ones, so they sum to 89.3% rather than 100%.
That's a deliberate choice and the kind that belongs on the page: excluding
them would silently inflate every sector by a ninth.

The chart takes plain data, not a series:

```ts
type CategoryDatum = { label: string; value: number };
// [{ label: 'N', value: 10.7 }, { label: 'NNE', value: 6.0 }, …]
```

## Build it

A categorical bar chart is `<BarChart categories>` plus a `<CategoryAxis>`, and
the ordinal axis is derived from the data rather than configured:

```tsx
<ChartContainer width={640} theme={theme} showAxis={false} cursor="crosshair">
  <ChartRow height={196}>
    <YAxis id="pct" side="left" label="% of hours" format=",.0f" width={46} />
    <Layers>
      <BarChart categories={rose} axis="pct" gap={6} />
    </Layers>
  </ChartRow>
  <CategoryAxis label="All of 2024" />
</ChartContainer>
```

Three details that aren't obvious:

- **`categories` replaces `series` and `column`.** Each datum carries its own
  value, so there's no column to name; passing both is a type error rather than
  a runtime surprise.
- **`showAxis={false}` on the container** suppresses the default _time_ axis.
  The container still owns an x scale, but it's an ordinal one now, and the
  `<CategoryAxis>` is what renders it. Leave the time axis on and you get two
  x axes, one of them meaningless.
- **The layer hands the axis its category names**, in data order. The axis
  doesn't need a list of sixteen labels configured into it — and because
  ordering is the data's, "clockwise from north" survives without a sort
  comparator that knows about compass points.

`gap={6}` is what separates sixteen bars in 640 px into countable objects. At
this count you can afford it; a 200-category chart wants `gap={0}` and a
different reading strategy.

To make the axis ceiling comparable across months — which is what the Gallery
card's month-by-month sweep needs — pin it to the tallest sector in _any_
month rather than letting each frame auto-fit:

```tsx
<YAxis id="pct" side="left" label="% of hours" width={46} min={0} max={21} />
```

That peak is June's SSW, at 21.0% of the month's hours. Without the pin, every
month would fill the plot to the same height and the sweep would say nothing at
all.

:::note Why not a polar rose?
A wind rose is traditionally drawn on a polar plot, and pond doesn't draw one.
The Cartesian version trades the compass metaphor for something a polar plot is
genuinely bad at: comparing two adjacent sectors' magnitudes, which is a length
comparison here and an area-and-angle comparison there. If you need the compass
metaphor, this isn't the library for that chart.
:::

## Options to try

| Option                            | What it does                                    | Reach for it when                                                 |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `orientation="horizontal"`        | Categories down the y axis, bars running across | Long labels — `NNE` is fine, `us-east-1a` is not                   |
| `binColors`                       | One colour per bar, in data order               | Colouring the four quadrants, or highlighting one sector           |
| `<BarList>`                       | A ranked table with bars, not a chart           | The categories are entities to be ranked, not slots on an axis     |
| `selected` / `onSelect`           | Click a bar; it stays lit                       | The bar is a filter for something else on the page                 |
| `<CategoryAxis label>`            | A caption under the ordinal axis                | Naming what the whole distribution is _of_ — the card uses it for the month |
| Recompute `rose` from a `month`   | Swaps the whole distribution in place           | Comparing periods — a `CategoryDatum` carries one value per slot, so two periods are two renders, not two layers |
